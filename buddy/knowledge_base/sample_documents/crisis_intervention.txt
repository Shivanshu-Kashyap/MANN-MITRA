Crisis Intervention Guide

Recognizing a Mental Health Crisis:
A mental health crisis occurs when a person's behavior puts them at risk of hurting themselves or others, or when they are unable to care for themselves or function effectively. Crises can be triggered by traumatic events, loss, overwhelming stress, or the escalation of an existing mental health condition.

Warning Signs of Suicidal Ideation:
- Talking about wanting to die or kill oneself
- Looking for ways to kill oneself (searching online, obtaining means)
- Talking about feeling hopeless or having no reason to live
- Talking about feeling trapped or in unbearable pain
- Talking about being a burden to others
- Increasing use of alcohol or drugs
- Acting anxious, agitated, or reckless
- Sleeping too little or too much
- Withdrawing or feeling isolated
- Showing rage or talking about seeking revenge
- Displaying extreme mood swings
- Giving away possessions
- Saying goodbye to people as if for the last time

Immediate Actions for Crisis:
1. STAY CALM - Your calm presence matters
2. LISTEN without judgment - Let the person talk
3. ASK DIRECTLY - "Are you thinking about suicide?" (This does NOT plant the idea)
4. KEEP THEM SAFE - Remove access to lethal means if possible
5. BE THERE - Don't leave them alone
6. HELP CONNECT - Guide them to professional help

Self-Harm Awareness:
Self-harm (non-suicidal self-injury) is when someone deliberately hurts themselves as a way of coping with emotional pain, anger, or frustration. Common forms include cutting, burning, hitting, or scratching.

Self-harm is NOT:
- A suicide attempt (though it increases risk)
- Attention-seeking behavior
- Something to be ashamed of
- Untreatable

Self-harm IS:
- A coping mechanism that has become harmful
- A sign that someone needs better coping strategies
- Treatable with professional help
- Something that deserves compassion, not judgment

Helping Someone Who Self-Harms:
- Don't panic or show shock
- Listen without judgment
- Avoid ultimatums ("stop or else")
- Express care and concern
- Encourage professional help
- Help identify alternative coping strategies (ice cubes, rubber band snap, drawing on skin)
- Know your limits - you don't have to handle this alone

Safety Planning:
A safety plan is a personalized, practical plan that anyone can use to stay safe during a crisis:
1. Warning signs that a crisis may be developing
2. Internal coping strategies (things I can do alone)
3. People and social settings that provide distraction
4. People I can ask for help (friends, family)
5. Professionals or agencies I can contact
6. Making my environment safe (removing means)

Post-Crisis Support:
- Follow up with the person regularly
- Help them connect with ongoing professional support
- Take care of your own mental health
- Don't promise to keep secrets about safety
- Document the incident if in a professional role
