Depression Awareness and Support Guide

What is Depression?
Depression (Major Depressive Disorder) is a common but serious mood disorder that affects how you feel, think, and handle daily activities. It is NOT a sign of weakness, and it is NOT something you can simply "snap out of." Depression is a medical condition that can be effectively treated.

Symptoms of Depression (DSM-5 Criteria):
At least 5 of the following symptoms present for at least 2 weeks:
1. Depressed mood most of the day, nearly every day
2. Markedly diminished interest or pleasure in activities (anhedonia)
3. Significant weight change or appetite change
4. Insomnia or excessive sleeping (hypersomnia)
5. Psychomotor agitation or retardation
6. Fatigue or loss of energy nearly every day
7. Feelings of worthlessness or excessive guilt
8. Difficulty thinking, concentrating, or making decisions
9. Recurrent thoughts of death or suicidal ideation

Depression in College Students:
- Affects approximately 40% of college students at some point
- Academic pressure is a leading contributor
- Often co-occurs with anxiety
- Can look different than "just being sad" — may present as irritability, anger, or numbness
- Social media can worsen symptoms through comparison
- Transition to college is a high-risk period

PHQ-9 Severity Levels:
- 0-4: Minimal depression
- 5-9: Mild depression
- 10-14: Moderate depression
- 15-19: Moderately severe depression
- 20-27: Severe depression

Coping with Depression:

Daily Habits That Help:
- Get out of bed at a consistent time (even if it's hard)
- Take a shower and get dressed
- Eat regular meals (nutrition affects mood)
- Move your body, even briefly (a 10-minute walk)
- Get natural sunlight (especially in the morning)
- Stay hydrated
- Connect with one person each day

Behavioral Activation:
Depression often creates a cycle: feel bad → do less → feel worse. Breaking this cycle:
- Identify activities that used to bring joy
- Start very small (5 minutes is enough)
- Schedule activities rather than waiting for motivation
- Track mood before and after activities
- Celebrate small accomplishments

Thought Patterns to Watch:
- "I'll always feel this way" → Depression is treatable and temporary
- "Nobody cares about me" → Depression distorts our perception of others
- "I'm a burden" → Reaching out shows strength, not weakness
- "Nothing will help" → Many effective treatments exist
- "I should be able to handle this alone" → Even the strongest people need support

Supporting Someone with Depression:
- Check in regularly (a simple text means a lot)
- Listen without trying to "fix" them
- Avoid saying "just cheer up" or "others have it worse"
- Offer to help with practical tasks
- Encourage professional help gently
- Be patient — recovery takes time
- Don't take their withdrawal personally
- Educate yourself about depression

Treatment Options:
1. Psychotherapy: CBT, Interpersonal Therapy (IPT), Psychodynamic Therapy
2. Medication: SSRIs, SNRIs (prescribed by psychiatrist or doctor)
3. Combination: Therapy + medication is often most effective
4. Lifestyle changes: Exercise, sleep, nutrition, social connection
5. Support groups: Peer support from others who understand
6. Campus counseling: Free or low-cost for students
7. Crisis resources: 988 Lifeline, Crisis Text Line (741741)

Recovery Is Possible:
- Depression is one of the most treatable mental health conditions
- 80-90% of people with depression respond well to treatment
- Recovery is not linear — setbacks are normal and expected
- Treatment works best when started early
- You deserve help and support
